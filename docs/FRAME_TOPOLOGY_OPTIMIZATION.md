# Vega structural frame topology optimization

The default RBCC/ejector assembly now has an **optimized load-path frame**. Vega builds a candidate ground structure of circumferential rings, axial rails and two diagonal directions around the protected duct. A three-dimensional axial-bar finite-element model solves two representative load cases: the requested rocket thrust distributed over the first frame ring, and a transverse load based on the larger of 10% of that thrust or 5% of the transition dynamic-pressure force on the inlet capture area. The last ring is fixed in translation. These loads and boundary conditions are declared model assumptions, not measured flight or engine loads.

The optimizer minimizes summed compliance (force times displacement) under a user-controlled member-volume fraction. Each candidate bar's cross-sectional area is a design variable; its area maps directly to the radius of the circular beam exported by Vega Geometry. The optimality-criteria update uses finite-element compliance sensitivities, a volume-constraint bisection and a concept radius floor. Bars at that floor are removed only when the remaining truss stays stable and outperforms a uniform-area frame at the same actual material volume. The solver records the selected topology, volume, compliance, member radii, load cases, support condition and iterations in the saved design. The resulting frame is one of the actual exported assembly bodies, so changing the volume fraction changes the mesh.

This is ground-structure **member-layout and sizing optimization** of the external frame. It does not remove the wall, cooling channels, injector, feed routes, or core pressure vessel. It does not certify a minimum-mass engine. A truss model does not capture beam bending, ring-shell coupling, joint stiffness, buckling, stress concentration, fatigue, thermal stress, vibration, or realistic airframe load paths. The selected 70,000 N/mm² isotropic elastic modulus is an explicit illustration; a real material and measured load cases are required before structural decisions. The radius floor is a geometric concept constraint, not a qualified additive-manufacturing rule.

The governing minimum-compliance / prescribed-volume ground-structure formulation follows the original [Ben-Tal and Bendsøe truss-topology research](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0803015). Unlike a continuum SIMP density field, Vega uses bar area directly so the finite-element stiffness and exported circular beam area agree. The current result is an unvalidated concept screen.
